Reading

This page is a running log of what I'm reading. Each entry gets a short note on why it was interesting and the core lessons I took from it — and at the bottom, what's next on the list.

My taste runs to non-fiction, especially biographies and autobiographies, and books that introduce new concepts for living a better life.

Recently read

  • Streetwise, by Lloyd Blankfein

    Traces his life up to his tenure leading Goldman Sachs — an impactful life, from overcoming poverty to the top of the financial world, steering through the biggest financial crisis of our time. I especially enjoyed his writing style and self-deprecating humor.

  • The NVIDIA Way, by Tae Kim

    The NVIDIA story. It shows Jensen Huang as a visionary, relentless leader whose seemingly overnight success — when demand for AI chips took off — was really decades of preparing for that very moment, with an unwavering focus on excellence throughout.

  • Made in America, by Sam Walton

    Sam Walton's life story — from his youth to founding Walmart and scaling it into the behemoth it is today. A great American story: anything is possible if you put your mind to it, surround yourself with great people, and get a bit of luck.

Up next

  • The New Contented Little Baby Book, by Gina Ford

    I'm a new dad and need all the help I can get!

  • Never Split the Difference, by Chris Voss

    I learned a lot in the negotiations class I took in business school, and I see negotiation as an important life skill. I suspect this book will reinforce a lot of my earlier learnings and offer fresh perspectives on the topic.

  • Grant, by Ron Chernow

    I've been on a binge of content about early American presidents. Ulysses Grant interests me: he fought in the Civil War, played a big role in the Reconstruction era, and faced corruption claims against his administration.
